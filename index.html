Abstract

Contactless respiratory monitoring from video is a promising alternative to wearable sensors but remains sensitive to motion artifacts and variations in viewpoint and lighting, which degrade the reliability of respiratory rate (RR) estimates. Traditional signal processing methods often struggle to separate physiological motion from visual artifacts, while large multimodal models (LMMs) can reason about these artifacts but are often too computationally demanding for privacy-sensitive or embedded deployments. This work aims to improve robustness and deployability by explicitly decoupling visual perception from downstream reasoning. We propose VLS-RR (Visual–Language–SLM Respiratory Rate) auditor, a three-stage framework where video segments are converted into fused motion signals and summary plots. A vision–language model (VLM) then produces a textual description of rhythm, breath count, and artifacts. Finally, small language models (SLMs) perform chain-of-thought (CoT) auditing over this evidence and the signal-derived RR candidates. We evaluate VLS-RR on a 50-video belt-synchronized dataset spanning diverse breathing patterns, viewpoints, and lighting conditions. Compared with signal-only baselines, VLS-RR reduces segment-level MAE by ≈ 30% and improves R² from 0.85 to 0.92. It also outperforms numeric-only SLM baselines with an additional ≈ 25% MAE reduction. Finally, on an embedded edge device, compact SLM auditors run with 0.82–1.71 s latency per 6 s window, indicating that decoupling perception and reasoning enables accurate, resource-efficient RR estimation from video.

Why VLS-RR

Signal-only estimation is fragile

Video-based respiratory-rate estimation is easily affected by motion, occlusion, lighting changes, viewpoint variation, and non-respiratory body movement, which can distort extracted breathing signals.

Visual evidence improves trustworthiness

Instead of relying only on numeric RR candidates, VLS-RR reasons over waveform regularity, motion spikes, breath-count cues, and agreement or disagreement across estimates.

Small models make deployment practical

By separating artifact interpretation from final decision-making, the framework enables compact SLM auditors to run efficiently in privacy-sensitive and embedded settings.

Method

Stage 1: Signal and artifact generation

Short chest-video segments are converted into fused respiratory motion signals together with diagnostic artifacts such as waveform plots, optical-flow summaries, and signal-derived RR candidates.

Stage 2: Visual evidence extraction

A vision-language model reads the generated plots and summarizes rhythm, breath count, motion spikes, and agreement or disagreement between candidate RR estimates.

Stage 3: SLM auditing

A small language model audits the visual evidence together with the numerical candidates and outputs a validated respiratory-rate estimate, confidence level, and reliability label.

Instead of repeating the full pipeline figure, this section highlights the role of each stage: generate interpretable artifacts, summarize them as textual evidence, and use lightweight reasoning to produce a more trustworthy final estimate.

Dataset and Collection Setup

We evaluate the framework on an in-house belt-synchronized dataset consisting of 50 RGB videos, each roughly 2.5–3.5 minutes long at 1920×1080 and 30 fps. The recordings cover multiple breathing patterns and viewpoints, including frontal, oblique, overhead, and supine settings. Ground-truth respiratory signals are obtained using a Vernier respiration belt synchronized with the video stream.

Figure 2. In-house dataset collection procedure. The dataset includes multiple postures and synchronized belt-based ground truth, along with ROI extraction from video for contactless respiratory analysis.

Results

The full VLS-RR pipeline improves substantially over classical signal-only baselines and over an SLM that sees only numerical candidates. In the full framework, MAE drops to 5.2 bpm and R² rises to 0.92 in the few-shot setting, compared with 7.4 bpm and 0.84 for the mean of the two classical estimates.

On-Device Efficiency and Accuracy–Efficiency Trade-Offs

To study embedded practicality, the full VLS-RR pipeline is profiled on NVIDIA Jetson Orin across different SLM auditors. Average latency per 6-second window ranges from approximately 0.82 seconds for MobileLLM-1B to 1.71 seconds for medgemma-4B-it. The paper also reports a smooth accuracy–efficiency trade-off, where moving from smaller 1B-scale auditors to stronger 3B–4B auditors improves MAE at the cost of higher latency and energy consumption.

Qualitative Example

Beyond aggregate metrics, the paper also inspects individual segments to show how VLS-RR resolves conflicting evidence. In the representative example below, Stage 1 produces discrepant candidates, the VLM reports rhythmic breathing with visible cycles together with non-respiratory spikes, and the SLM auditor selects the peak-counting value while labeling the segment as noisy but usable with medium confidence.

Figure 5. A representative segment where VLS-RR resolves conflicting estimates through visual analysis and structured reasoning instead of relying on a single output.

Why this matters. The goal is not only to predict a respiratory rate, but also to indicate whether the evidence is coherent enough to trust that estimate. This makes the framework more interpretable and more suitable for practical deployment than a single-pass predictor that outputs only one number.
